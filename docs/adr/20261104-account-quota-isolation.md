# Account-scoped quota failures and continuation

Status: accepted, 2026-11-04. The operator requested that confirmed unavailable accounts not prevent other accounts' quota queries. One independent decision review accepted all seven criteria after clarifying quota-only restrictions and status-code evidence. Implementation and live acceptance are separate.

Keep account restrictions and provider admission distinct. An explicit account-scoped protocol refusal blocks that account; HTTP 401/403 alone and unclassified discovery errors do not establish scope. Shared or unknown failures retain the provider pause. Never replay an unknown send or change provider egress. Quota exhaustion restricts sending but still permits reading quota.

Store operator-disabled status with existing account profiles, preserve identity, credentials and prior observations, and enforce matching identity restrictions at both Manager and shared quota admission on the owning machine. Re-enabling is local and does not remove other holds or issue requests. Separate the latest quota batch from the send batch, retain per-account results across subsequent operations and restart, and confirm the exact eligible remaining subset before continuation through the existing Monitor route bridge. No new scheduler or credential store is introduced.

New pauses retain their originating account operation. An operator may explicitly attribute a current pause to an already disabled account only for an actually attempted quota-only failure, with matching pause and operation, no active work, no conflicting source or unknown-send/login-rate evidence. Keep the original pause and the operator attribution; this is a scope decision, not proof that the provider or network recovered. Legacy unbound pauses are never attributed automatically. Existing unrelated restrictions remain. Historical operations without a quota batch ID remain historical operations; a new explicitly selected subset can continue their unfinished work without inventing a past batch.

Alternatives rejected: ignoring every failure could expose accounts to shared faults; pausing everything reproduces the observed partial-query blockage; recovering a confirmed unavailable account is not a prerequisite for querying independent accounts; a second scheduler or account database duplicates existing owners. This refines the failure scope of the shared execution and recovery decisions while retaining their identity, confirmation, observation and egress contracts.

Acceptance covers persistent account refusal with a successful following query, unknown/shared failure stopping unattempted work, disabled identity admission across consumers, accurate batch results and continuation, stale scope rejection, quota-only eligibility, and preservation of send protection. Synthetic results do not establish provider acceptance or the cause of an account ban.
